### 1. LEAD — Xi, Trump and Putin hold the first three-way summit in Shenzhen
- Presidents Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin met in Shenzhen on
  Thursday 19 Nov — the first trilateral summit of the three leaders, held on
  the sidelines of the APEC leaders' meeting. They sat for a 90-minute meeting
  at Kylin Villa, a government guesthouse in the hills of Shenzhen.
- The meeting ended with a joint "peace declaration": the three nuclear powers
  "reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought" and
  agreed to "conduct a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing
  proliferation" — with no time frame and no plan for a trilateral arms
  reduction treaty.
- The declaration also pledged UN reform, more refined petroleum supplies and
  respect for state officials' immunity in international courts. It did NOT
  mention Russia's war in Ukraine or the US conflict with Iran; Putin said
  afterwards it set no ambitious goals and nobody would be forced to comply.
- Trump called the summit "bigger than Yalta" and told NBC it "went great"
  ("On a scale of zero to 10, I think it was a 12").
- Verification status (audit 20 Nov 2026): VERIFIED — no change.

### 2. JUSTICE — World Court finds Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya
- The International Court of Justice (ICJ) in The Hague ruled on Thursday
  19 Nov that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya Muslim minority
  in its 2017 "clearance operations" in northern Rakhine State — the first
  time in the court's 80-year history it has held a state responsible for
  genocide, and only the fourth time since the Nazi Holocaust that an
  international court has found the legal threshold for genocide met.
- The court found Myanmar's security forces killed about 10,000 Rohingya
  civilians, carried out mass executions of men and boys, widespread sexual
  violence, extreme violence against children, and burned or razed hundreds of
  villages. The case was brought by The Gambia in 2019.
- Presiding Judge Yuji Iwasawa: "the only reasonable inference ... is an
  intention to destroy" the Rohingya in northern Rakhine State. The ruling is
  final and cannot be appealed, but the court has limited power to enforce it.
  The court ordered Myanmar to make reparations, including facilitating safe,
  voluntary and dignified return.
- Verification status (audit 20 Nov 2026): VERIFIED — no change.

### 3. UKRAINE — Kerch Bridge hit for a third time in four days
- Ukrainian forces struck the Kerch Bridge (the road-and-rail link between
  Russia and occupied Crimea) for the third time in four days, overnight on
  18–19 Nov, this time hitting the railway section with Neptune coastal
  missiles, near the arch and the tracks. Train traffic was halted while
  damage was assessed; some rail traffic resumed on Thursday afternoon.
  Russia's transport ministry said there were no casualties.
- The strikes on 16 and 17 Nov had already collapsed a section of the road
  bridge, including part of its main arch, into the Kerch Strait. The bridge
  is 19 km long and a key supply route for Crimea.
- President Zelenskyy described the strikes as part of an "unprecedented
  operation" using Ukrainian-made weapons, in response to Russian strikes on
  Ukrainian infrastructure. Ukraine also said it hit two oil refineries in
  southern Russia (Ilsky and Afipsky, Krasnodar region).
- Verification status (audit 20 Nov 2026): VERIFIED — no change.

### 4. MARKETS — Anthropic's record IPO debuts on Nasdaq

### 5. ARCTIC — Denmark approves the Greenland security deal, 109–0
- Denmark's parliament, the Folketing, voted 109–0 on Thursday 19 Nov to
  approve the defence and security agreement between the United States,
  Denmark and Greenland — the final parliamentary step for the deal, which
  paves the way for more US bases in Greenland and aims to defuse months of
  tension over President Trump's pressure for a possible US takeover of the
  mineral-rich island.
- The agreement was signed in New York on 22 September by Trump and the
  Danish and Greenlandic prime ministers, and supplements a 1951 defence
  accord. Greenland's own parliament approved it on 3 November (16–0, with
  seven abstentions). It enters into force once Denmark formally notifies the
  United States by diplomatic note; it needs no US Senate vote.
- Verification status (audit 20 Nov 2026): VERIFIED — no change.

### 6. MIDDLE EAST — Israel concludes its Gaza tank raid beyond the Yellow Line
- Israel's military said on Thursday 19 Nov it had concluded a tank raid in
  central Gaza to dismantle a suspected Hamas tunnel — the first ground
  incursion across the "Yellow Line" ceasefire boundary since the October
  2025 truce halted major fighting. The Salah al-Din road was reopened and
  displaced families began returning to the Nuseirat camp area.
- The raid began on Wednesday 18 Nov: tanks rolled onto the main north-south
  road near the Nuseirat and Bureij refugee camps, the first time since the
  truce that Israeli troops publicly announced a raid deep beyond the line
  (which leaves Israel holding more than 60% of Gaza). Hamas called it a
  flagrant ceasefire violation; Israel said the tunnel posed an immediate
  threat to its troops. Thousands of people fled during the raid.
- Verification status (audit 20 Nov 2026): VERIFIED — no change.
